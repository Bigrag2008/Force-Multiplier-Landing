The Second Number

A boy who could read a whole field never got to see the play that beat him. Now he can.

At eighteen, Marcus Ellison signed a plea in a room with a humming light, because he wanted to go home. He went home that night. He never really got free again. Then a school bell rings, and he opens his eyes on graduation day on the West Side of Dayton, with a body that doesn't hurt yet and a new way of seeing: the second number in every deal, the one nobody ever offered his mother.

The payday loan, the refinance, the rent-to-own couch, the "we buy houses" letter. Every trap in the book is real. Marcus can see them all now. The question is what he does with that, and what it costs him not to become the thing he's reading.

A note on the numbers. The figures in the book reflect documented predatory practices: payday and title lending, subprime and cash-out refinancing, rent-to-own markups, for-profit-college debt, tax-foreclosure offers, check-cashing and buy-here-pay-here fees. Names and a few West Dayton places are invented; the machine is not.

Then play it

The Second Number is also a cooperative board game for 1 to 5 players, set on the same blocks. Read every deal through red, amber and blue glasses, teach your neighbors to read them, and drag the one machine behind them all into the light. Its four-chapter Book One campaign follows the novel's story.
