The story

Graduation day, again

At eighteen, Marcus Ellison signed a plea in a room with a humming light, because he wanted to go home. Then a school bell rings, and he opens his eyes on graduation day on the West Side of Dayton, with a body that doesn't hurt yet and a new way of seeing: the second number in every deal, the one nobody ever offered his mother.

Marcus can see every trap on the block now. The question is what he does with that, and what it costs him not to become the thing he's reading.
