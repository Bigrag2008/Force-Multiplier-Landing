# How to Play The Second Number

*A cooperative game for 1 to 5 players, ages 12 and up. Your first game takes about 25 minutes.*

This is the easy guide: everything you need to play, in plain words, with examples. The full rulebook (RULES.md) has every detail, but you don't need it to start.

---

## Part 1 · Learn it in 10 minutes

### What the game is about
Every deal has two numbers. The **first number** is the one they show you: "$375 today!" The **second number** is the one they hide: the fee, the deadline, the interest, who's really making money.

You and your friends are neighbors on the West Side of Dayton. Deals keep showing up: payday loans, rent-to-own couches, "free" first months. Behind all of them is one **Machine** that squeezes the neighborhood.

Your job, **together**: learn to read the second number, teach each other, and wake the neighborhood up before the Machine takes it over. Everybody wins together or loses together.

### The six words you need

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Read** | How well you can read deals in one neighborhood (0 to 3). Read 1 shows the cost. Read 2 shows the deadline. Read 3 shows who's behind it. |
| **Trust** | Your energy for helping people. You spend it to teach and to bargain. (0 to 5) |
| **Shady** | What you pick up when you take a shortcut. Too much and you go over to the Machine. |
| **Squeeze** | How hard the Machine is gripping a neighborhood (0 to 3). At 3, that neighborhood is **captured**. |
| **Clarity** | How awake a neighborhood is (0 to 3). At 3, it's **awake**, and the Machine can't touch it. |
| **Props** | Respect you earn by lifting people up. The player with the most at the end is the table's MVP. |

The neighborhoods are called **districts**: Lending, Housing, Labor, Health and Education.

### How a deal card works
Every deal card has a **front** that everyone can see, and three hidden layers. You can only read as deep as your Read in that district:

| Layer | What it tells you | You need |
|---|---|---|
| Front | The pitch: "Rent is only $950!" | nothing |
| 🔴 Red | **The cost.** Is it a trap or an honest deal? What does it really cost? | Read 1 |
| 🟠 Amber | **The clock.** The deadline, and what happens when you miss it. | Read 2 |
| 🔵 Blue | **The connection.** Who's really behind it. | Read 3 |

The red layer also shows the trap's **lever**: the layer you have to be able to read to beat it (red, amber or blue).

On paper, you read the hidden layers through **Grandma's red reading glasses** (the red lens). On the website, you drag the glasses over the card.

**Example.** The front says *"Quick Cash: $375 today. One flat fee: $56.25."* With Read 1 you see the red layer: *"That fee is a 391% yearly rate."* Now you know it's a trap.

### Your three choices
When you face a deal, you pick one:

| Choice | On an **honest** deal | On a **trap** |
|---|---|---|
| **TAKE** it | Real help! +2 Trust, +1 Clarity, +1 Props. | **The shortcut.** It helps the neighborhood right now (−2 Squeeze, or +2 Clarity if there's no Squeeze), but you take **+2 Shady**. |
| **WALK** away | You turned down real help. −1 Trust. | Safe. Nothing happens. |
| **NEGOTIATE** (costs 1 Trust) | You haggled away real help. | If your Read reaches the trap's lever, you **beat it**: −1 Squeeze, +1 Clarity, +2 Props. If not, **you got played**: +1 Squeeze. |

**The big idea:** seeing a trap isn't the same as beating it. Read 1 tells you it's a trap. To bargain it down, your Read has to reach its lever.

**Can't read it at all?** At Read 0 you only see the front. Most deals are traps, but walking away from an honest one costs Trust. That's why you study.

### The shortcut, and the Fall
TAKE on a trap is really tempting: it's the fastest way to save a district that's about to be captured. But every shortcut puts Shady on you. **At Shady 6, you Fall**: you go over to the Machine, and on your turns the Machine moves through you. Your friends can bring you back.

Before you take a shortcut, **say the price out loud**: *"Taking this saves Housing, but I get +2 Shady, and I'm already at 4."* Then decide together.

### Your first game: the Block Party (25 minutes)
Start here. It uses just three districts, **Lending, Housing and Education**, and leaves everything else in the box.

**Setup.** Everyone starts with **2 Trust**, **0 Shady** and **Read 0** everywhere. Put the round marker on round 1.

**On your turn, take 2 actions** (3 if you're playing alone). Pick from:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Study** | +1 Read in a district. +1 Props. |
| **Face a deal** | Draw a deal from a district, read what you can, then TAKE, WALK or NEGOTIATE. |
| **Teach** | Give a teammate +1 Read in a district where you read better than they do. Costs 1 Trust. +2 Props. |
| **Gather** | Hang out with the block: +2 Trust (up to 5), −1 Shady. |

**Teaching tip (call and response).** When you teach, read a deal's red layer out loud. If your learner says the second number back to you, you each get +1 Props.

**After everyone's turn, the Machine strikes.** It hits the district that's closest to waking up: **+1 Squeeze and −1 Clarity** there. How many times it strikes depends on the round:

| Players | Strikes in rounds 1 to 8 |
|---|---|
| 1 or 2 | 0, 1, 1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 1 |
| 3 | 1, 2, 2, 2, 1, 2, 2, 2 |
| 4 | 3 every round |
| 5 | 4 every round |

**You win** the moment **2 of the 3 districts are awake** (Clarity 3).
**You lose** if **2 districts are captured** (Squeeze 3), or if round 8 ends.

**Shortcuts in the Block Party.** Taking a trap gives you 2 Shady chips, and helps a little less (+1 Clarity instead of +2). At 3 chips, hand them back and **sit out your next turn**. Nobody Falls in the Block Party.

When you've won a Block Party, you're ready for the full game.

## Part 2 · The full game (45 to 60 minutes)

### What's different
- All **five districts**: Lending, Housing, Labor, Health, Education.
- Each player has a **Role** with a special power.
- A **Scenario** sets the starting trouble.
- A hidden **Owner** is running the Machine. Figure out which one it is.
- **Gathering places** (the Commons), each with a bonus.
- **Patterns**: when the Machine grips several districts at once, it gets worse.
- **The Underworld**: the final act, where you expose the Owner.

### Setup
1. Pick a **Scenario**. For your first full game, use *The Audit* or *A Quiet Year*.
2. Shuffle the 4 **Owner** cards. Put one face-down under the board without looking.
3. Each player picks a **Role**, and starts with 2 Trust, 0 Shady and Read 0 everywhere. (The Elder starts with Read 1 everywhere.)
4. Find your table size:

| Players | Actions per turn | Machine strikes per round |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 1, 1, 1, then 2 (repeat) |
| 2 | 3 | 1, then 2 (repeat) |
| 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 2 | 1, then 2 (repeat) |
| 5 | 2 | 2, then 3 (repeat) |

The strikes are printed on the round track, so you don't have to remember them.

### The roles

| Role | Special power |
|---|---|
| **The Translator** | Teaches for free. |
| **The Student** | Learns double: +2 Read when taught. |
| **The Elder** | Starts with Read 1 everywhere. Once a game, can **Teach the History** of a district: it wakes up instantly. Read the History card out loud. |
| **The Veteran** | Blocks two Machine strikes per game. |
| **The Organizer** | Beating a trap drops Squeeze by 2 instead of 1. |

### A round, step by step
1. **Start of the round:** once per game, you may call **the Cookout** instead of taking turns (see below).
2. **Everyone takes their turn**, one player after another.
3. **The Sou-Sou** (optional): the block can save together (see below).
4. **Check the board:** if all the Clarity on the board adds up to **9 or more**, the Underworld opens.
5. **The Machine moves:** it strikes, it muddies, then the Scenario, the Owner and any Patterns act.
6. **Check:** did you lose? If not, start the next round.

### All the actions
| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Study** | +1 Read in a district. +1 Props. |
| **Face a deal** | Draw a deal from a district. Read it. TAKE, WALK or NEGOTIATE. |
| **Teach** | +1 Read for a teammate where you read better. 1 Trust (free for the Translator). +2 Props. |
| **Gather** at a place | +2 Trust, −1 Shady, plus that place's bonus. |
| **Reach** | Bring a Fallen friend home. Costs 2 Trust. They come back with 4 Shady. +3 Props. |
| **Trace a thread** | Only after the Underworld opens. Needs Read 3 in an awake district. +2 Props. |
| **Expose** | Once you've traced enough threads: **you win**. |

### The gathering places (the Commons)
Every time you gather, you go somewhere real on the West Side. You get +2 Trust and −1 Shady, plus:

| Place | Bonus |
|---|---|
| **The Barbershop & Beauty Shop** | Your next Teach this turn is free. |
| **The Church** | −2 Shady instead of −1. |
| **Adventure Central** (Wesleyan MetroPark) | Your next Study this turn gives +2 Read. |
| **Gem City Market** (the co-op grocery) | Your next deal this turn: draw 2, pick 1. |
| **Grandma's Kitchen Table** | Once a game: everyone gets +2 Trust. |

### What the Machine does
- **It strikes** the districts closest to waking up: +1 Squeeze each, **never the same district twice in one round**.
- **It muddies** your best unfinished district: −1 Clarity.
- **An awake district (Clarity 3) is safe** from the Machine, until the Underworld opens.
- **Patterns:** when every district in a set is at Squeeze 2+ (Housing and Lending: **Redlining**), flip that Pattern card and read it aloud. It squeezes harder each round until you pull one of its districts below 2.

### The hidden Owner
One of four Owners is under the board, and it acts from round 1. Watch the board and guess which:
- **The Algorithm:** one extra strike every round.
- **The Lobbyist:** leans on your strongest unfinished district every round.
- **The Debt Buyer:** shortcuts cost 1 more Shady, and anyone carrying Shady loses Trust each round.
- **The Recruiter:** each round, a random player loses 1 Read somewhere.

### Coming together
- **The Cookout** (once a game): instead of taking turns, the whole block eats and compares paperwork. Everyone gets +2 Trust, −1 Shady, +1 Props, and catches up to the best reader at the table in one district.
- **The Sou-Sou** (a savings circle): after everyone's turn, each player with Trust may put 1 into the pot. When the pot reaches 4, the next player in the circle uses it to **clear 2 Squeeze** from any district, with no Shady. It's the honest payday loan.
- **The Green Book** (once a game): when a strike would capture a district, open it and that strike lands nowhere, like the guide Black travelers used to find safe routes.

### The Underworld: how you win
When the board's Clarity adds up to 9, **flip the Owner card**. Now it fights in the open: an extra strike every round, and it can knock an awake district back down.

To win, **trace 3 blue threads** (2 if you're playing alone). Each thread needs a player with **Read 3** in an **awake** district. Then take the **Expose** action. The block wins.

### How you lose
- **2 districts are captured** (Squeeze 3), or
- **everyone has Fallen**, or
- **round 12 ends**.

### Scoring
Win or lose, the player with the most **Props** is the one who lifted the most people. You never get Props for taking shortcuts.

> *How high did you climb before you turned into the thing you were fighting?*

## Part 3 · Other ways to play

### Solo: just you
Finished the book and want to play tonight? Choose **1 player**.
- You get **5 actions** a turn (3 in the Block Party).
- There's nobody at the table to teach, so you **Teach a neighbor**: spend 1 Trust in a district where you have Read 1 or more, and it gets **+1 Clarity** (and you get +2 Props). That's how one reader wakes a whole district.
- You only need to trace **2 threads** to win.

### Overlord: one player is the Machine
One player plays against everyone else. There's no hidden Owner: the Overlord chooses the Machine's moves each round (strike, muddy or tempt, which takes 1 Trust), following the same "never the same district twice" rule. The computer can play the Overlord too.
- **Counterfeits:** the Overlord has 3 fake "honest" deals it can slip onto a pile. They look like real help until you have **Read 3**. TAKE one and you're conned (+1 Squeeze, −1 Trust).
- **"Is that your handwriting?"** Once a game, a player may ask the Overlord if they touched the last deal's district this round. It costs 1 Trust, and the Overlord must answer truthfully.

### House rules (optional extras)
Turn any of these on once your table knows the game:
- **Fine-print dice:** roll a die to decide where a trap's lever is (1–3 red, 4–5 amber, 6 blue), so nobody can memorize the deck.
- **The Terrell rule:** being taught gives you Read but not protection. If you confront a trap there before the block has your back, it backfires (+1 Squeeze). Gathering anywhere covers you.
- **Endless deals:** the numbers on deals change every time, so you have to do the math yourself.
- **Work it out (the math variant):** with endless deals, figure out the second number before you look under the glasses. Great for classrooms.
- **Close reads:** one layer short of a trap's lever? Roll a die; a 5 or 6 talks them down.
- **"Render?":** once (and again after the Cookout), a teammate who reads a district better can read a deal for you.
- **Sign-now clocks** (on by default): some deals push you to "sign now!" Count down from 15. The deadline is always fake: when it runs out, nothing happens.

### The campaigns: one story, many games
Play a series of games where what happens carries forward. Each **chapter** is one game with its own setup and twist. Play them in order as one family, or play any chapter on its own.

**The Long Ledger** follows one West Dayton family across six eras:

| Chapter | Era | The twist |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Freedom's Ledger | 1865–1877 | Nobody starts able to read. The Freedman's Savings Bank looks like honest help until Read 3. |
| 2 · Up North | 1910s–1940s | The family just arrived: Health and Education open in round 3. |
| 3 · The Map | 1930s–1960s | Redlining from day one. Sign the League together to fight back. |
| 4 · Voice | 1955–1970 | Call the March once a game. Watch for deals meant to buy off organizers. |
| 5 · The Plants Close | 1990s–2009 | Choose: defend what you built, or let one district go. |
| 6 · Today | 2018– | The History your family lived comes back, and the Owner shows every face it wore. |

Between chapters, the family keeps:
- **The Family Ledger**, a score from −10 to +10 (wins and awake districts add; losses and captured districts subtract).
- **Stickers** on the districts: woken ones are **Rebuilt** (they start stronger), captured ones are **Scarred** (they start weaker).
- **A Family Decision** after each chapter (go north now or save one more season? stay or move?) that changes the next chapter.
- If you lose a chapter, **the community rallies**: everyone starts the next one with +1 Trust.

**Book One** follows the novel *The Second Number* in four chapters:

| Chapter | The twist |
|---|---|
| 1 · The Chain | A Block Party where every lesson gets passed on to one more person. |
| 2 · The Net | A county job at the Desk: easy Trust, but you can't face deals or teach while you're there. |
| 3 · The Crown | The Overlord offers someone a crown that keeps them from Falling, while everyone else pays for it. |
| 4 · The Empty Throne | Nobody can Expose the Owner until everyone has taught. No single leader. |

**Playing on paper?** Every chapter has a printed **envelope** with its rules page, era deal cards and reference cards. The first chapters include the family sheets and stickers.

## Part 4 · Everything the website does

Play free in any browser at the game's website. Nothing to install, no accounts.

- **Learn to play:** a 30-second comic tells the story, then the **Coach** walks your first game with you. It glows on the move it would make and says why. Turn it off any time.
- **Play on everyone's phones:** one device is the table; everyone joins with a four-letter code and plays their own seat. Deal cards stay private on the reader's phone.
- **It saves itself.** Close the tab and pick up exactly where you left off.
- **Sound and access:** sound you can mute; plays with a keyboard alone; reads every move to screen readers; **Plain cards** if the red lens doesn't work for you; colors that work for color-blind players.
- **En español:** switch to Spanish at the bottom of any page. Each phone at a table can choose its own language.
- **Solo**, **Overlord**, the **campaigns** and every **house rule** are in the setup screen.
- **The dollar count:** at the end of every game, the points turn back into real money: what the block signed, what it walked away from, and what it stayed out of.
- **How did it go?** An optional three-tap question at the end of a game helps make the game better. No names.
- **Endless deals:** practice reading fresh deals, one at a time.
- **Read your own deal:** type in a real offer (a phone plan, a car loan) and see its second number and its real yearly rate. Nothing you type leaves your device.
- **A block that remembers:** play the full game again as the same block, and the next game starts where this one left off.
- **Family codes:** move a campaign family to another device by copying a short code.
- **Your City editions:** make the game about your own side of town: your neighborhoods, gathering places, local history, local deals, real help nearby, and your own pictures.
- **For teachers:** the **Teacher's guide** (one class period, two printable pages) and the **Teacher's view**, which adds up a whole class's games from short codes students copy at the end. No accounts.
- **Print and play:** free PDFs of the full kit (cards, board, rules) and the teacher's guide, in English and Spanish, and the chapter envelopes (in English).
