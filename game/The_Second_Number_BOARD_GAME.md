# THE SECOND NUMBER — The Board Game
### A design brief

*A cooperative game where everyday people learn to see the hidden cost in every deal — and teach a whole block to see it too. Teaching, fun, and the Black American experience on the same wavelength, built to travel the world.*

**Working titles:** *The Second Number* · *Read the Machine* · *Wake the Block*
**Part of the Individual Democracy / Civic Armor universe.**

---

## THE ONE LINE
You can't win by beating the other players. You win by teaching them to see — and the first time someone holds the red lens up to a card and gasps, the whole lesson lands without a word.

## WHY IT MATTERS (the soul)
Most games teach you to bankrupt your friends (Monopoly literally celebrates it). This one does the opposite: it teaches people to protect each other from the machine that's really coming for all of them. It's rooted in the specific Black American experience of navigating predatory systems — the payday loan, the rent-to-own, the for-profit school, the second price nobody showed you — and *because* it's that specific and that true, it goes universal. The machine is everywhere; every culture has its version. This is a game that belongs on the table at the cookout **and** in the classroom. Specific enough to be real. True enough to be global.

---

## THE WEST DAYTON EDITION
The game is set on the **West Side of Dayton, Ohio**, and built from Black American culture. Each district is a part of the West Side, from Wright-Dunbar to Madden Hills, after the plants closed, and the hospital gone. The block gathers at the barbershop on West Third Street, the church, Adventure Central at Wesleyan MetroPark, Gem City Market and Grandma's kitchen table. It saves together in the **Sou-Sou**, calls the **Cookout** once a game, keeps one **Green Book** for a safe route, teaches by **call and response**, and the Elder tells true stories from the **History deck**: the Contract Buyers League, Dunbar and the Wright brothers, Mayor James H. McGee. The lens is Grandma's reading glasses; the role cards are church hand fans.

See `ROADMAP.md` for what changed, `ART_DIRECTION.md` for the look, `CAMPAIGN.md` for the Legacy campaign across six eras, and `COMMUNITY.md` for the people, partners, permissions and fact-checks behind it.

## THE SHOWSTOPPER MECHANIC: THE LENS
Every **Deal card** (loan, lease, job, tuition, car note, hospital bill) has:
- a **price you can see**, printed in black on the front, and
- a **second number** — the real cost — printed in **red** that is *invisible to the naked eye*.

The box comes with a **red decoder lens.** You cannot read the second number until you've earned the **Read** to use the lens on that type of deal. New players are blind to what's being taken from them. As they develop Read, the hidden cost *appears.*

**Seeing is not beating.** Read 1 (red) tells you a deal is a trap. But every trap carries a small **lever key** in its corner — red, amber, or blue — the layer that holds the leverage to beat it. To *negotiate* it down you need Read that deep. Spotting the payday loan's 391% is the first step; beating the "free first month" means reading the clawback clock (amber); beating the debt-relief mill means naming who's behind it (blue).

> Information asymmetry = power asymmetry, made physical. You literally cannot see what the machine takes until you learn to look.

## THE PLAYERS (the village)
Cooperative, 2–5 players. Each picks a **role** with a unique gift — because no one sees the whole machine alone, and you *need* each other:

- **The Translator** — teaches for free (you still have to read a district better than your learner to teach it); the bridge that spreads Read fastest.
- **The Student** — gains double Read when taught; the hungry one.
- **The Elder** — starts with Read 1 everywhere; once per game, "Teach the History" wakes a whole district.
- **The Veteran** — shields the block from two Machine strikes per game (came home to protect it).
- **The Organizer** — a successful negotiation drops Squeeze by 2, not 1 (collective action); rallies the block.

*(The five roles as built. There is no separate "Builder" — the Commons is an action any player can take, not a role.)*

## THE OPPONENT: THE MACHINE
A central engine/tower that grows each round and plays **Extraction cards** on the five districts — **Housing, Lending, Labor, Health, Education.** It runs semi-automatically (like the virus in Pandemic), so everyone's playing *against the board, together.*

## YOUR TURN: EXIT · VOICE · LOYALTY
Every deal is the old choice in miniature — **Exit** (WALK), **Voice** (NEGOTIATE), **Loyalty** (TAKE). On your turn you take 2–3 actions (Study, Face a deal, Teach, gather at the Commons, Reach a Fallen friend, and in the endgame Trace the Underworld). You don't spend money — you spend **Time** (your actions) and **Trust**, and you earn **Clarity**. **Trust is the real currency:** every negotiation and every lesson costs one, it caps at 5, and the Commons is where you earn it back.

## THE TWIST THAT MAKES IT *YOURS*: THE SHADY PATH
There's a tempting shortcut. You *can* get rich and powerful fast by using the machine's own tools — **Take** cards: leverage, pressure, squeezing people. They're marked. Play too many and your piece **flips into a Machine piece.** And if you "win" the board that way — surprise, you **lose.** You became the boss.

> The rulebook's last line: *"How high did you climb before you turned into the thing you were fighting?"*

A board game you can win by selling out — where winning that way is secretly the loss. Nobody has ever made that. That's the whole philosophy as a rule.

## THE FALLEN — the corruption path, fully playable (the most electric moment in the game)
Flipping isn't a quiet exit anymore. When a player corrupts out, they **don't leave the table — they stay in the turn order and the Machine starts moving through them.** The Fallen gets real enemy actions:
- **Extract** — feed the Machine, push a district's pressure toward capture.
- **Obscure** — bury the second number again: pull Read back out of a district the block woke up.
- **Tempt** — whisper a sweeter deal to a teammate and drain their Trust.

Now the betrayal is *live.* The person who was carrying the crew turns and starts tearing down the exact districts they helped wake. It's the table's biggest gut-punch — and it's genuinely fun to *be* the one who turned.

**The counter-tension — redemption.** A Fallen player is never gone. The crew can spend 2 hard-won Trust and an action to **reach** them and pull them home (the rescuer earns +3 Props — saving a soul is the most legendary thing you can do). But every turn spent reaching is a turn not spent defending the board. Save the district, or save your friend?

**The guardrail that protects the whole thesis:** the Fallen can **win the board** — if they drag the block down to two captured districts, the Machine wins the game. **But they can never win the Props.** The scoreboard that actually crowns a winner only counts people lifted, districts woken, souls reached. When the Fallen "wins," the game says it plainly: *"You won the board as the Machine — and earned 0 Props. The Owner just wore your face. You win nothing by becoming it."* The fall is thrilling, real, and playable — but it is still a fall. **You don't win by becoming the thing you fight.** That's the philosophy, enforced by the rules.

## OPTIONAL: OVERLORD MODE — one player IS the Machine (1-vs-many)
For groups who want the full competitive, head-to-head version, there's an optional way to play: **one player takes the Machine from the start, openly — The Overlord.** No hidden Owner, no slow corruption; it's the block versus a live human adversary. The Overlord takes Machine actions every round (extract, obscure, tempt), choosing targets with a mind instead of a deck. The block still wins by dragging the Underworld into the light; the Overlord wins by capturing two districts. It's a different, nastier game on the same board — and it's **optional, so it never limits the co-op experience either way.** Toggle it on when the table wants a villain with a face.

*(Both are live in the prototype: the Fallen plays as the Machine on their turn with redemption as the counter-pull — `python3 second_number_play.py --auto` — and Overlord mode runs with `--overlord`. Overlord moves obey the same spread rule as the Machine — one move per district per round — so no one can snipe a district in a single round.)*

## HOW YOU WIN / LOSE
- **Win (together):** wake districts until total Clarity cracks the Underworld open (9), then **trace 3 blue threads** (Read 3 in a woken district) while the Owner fights in the open, and **expose** it.
- **Lose:** the Machine captures **two** districts, **or** every player corrupts into a Machine, **or** round 12 ends — the amber clock is real.

*(As built and balance-tested across 2–5 players with every West Dayton mechanic in play: co-op 54–68% block wins, Overlord 46–59%, zero first-round losses; careful play beats both "never take a shortcut" and "take every shortcut". See `balance_report.txt` and `RULES.md`.)*

## THE SECRET: IT ACTUALLY TEACHES
Every Deal card is a **real trap** — the payday-loan APR, the rent-to-own markup, the for-profit-school debt, the car-note payment trick, the hospital "opening offer." **The "Read the Machine" content series literally becomes the card deck.** A kid who plays this gets genuinely better at spotting real-world scams. That makes it financial and civic literacy smuggled inside a game people play *for fun.* It's not merch — it's another jacket of the mission, a spiritual technology you can put on a shelf.

## THE UNDERWORLD (the hidden layer — the wildcard that connects everything)
Beneath the five visible districts is a **hidden under-board** — *The Underworld* — that cracks open once the block's total Clarity reaches 9, and can only be *exposed* by players who've reached the deepest level of Read (blue). It's the secret engine of the whole machine, and it carries the game's biggest truth:

- **The districts look separate. The Underworld reveals they're one machine.** Down there, the same money, the same firms, the same hands own a piece of all five domains. (This is the "Knowledge Graph" from the philosophy — how institutions are secretly connected — made playable.)
- It's ruled by **The Owner** — faceless (straight out of the novel) — the hand that moves between all domains and influences everything from below.
- **Wildcard mechanic:** Underworld events ripple across **all five districts at once**, because down there they're connected. One move below hits the whole board above.
- **Where the fallen go:** players who fully corrupt and flip to the Machine operate *from* the Underworld — that's where the Machine's real power sits.
- **The true endgame:** you *think* you're fighting five separate problems. Then someone gets deep enough to open the Underworld, and the whole table sees it's **one thing.** Truly winning isn't just waking the five districts — it's dragging the Underworld into the light, exposing the connection. The master level of the Read isn't seeing one trap. It's seeing that all the traps are the same trap.

That's the novel's climax and the philosophy's core, built as the hidden heart of the board.

## MACHINE PATTERNS (the harm is never one district — and it has a name)
The real world doesn't hurt you one domain at a time. It hurts you in **combinations**, and those combinations have names people half-know but can't quite see. So when the Machine grips two or three districts at once, the board unlocks a **named Machine Pattern** — the actual structural harm a community feels — and the back of each pattern card carries the real-world explainer, so players walk away knowing the word for what's being done to them.

**The pairs:**
- **Housing + Lending → REDLINING** — credit and housing denied by geography; your address decides what money costs you.
- **Health + Lending → THE MEDICAL-DEBT SPIRAL** — one illness becomes a debt becomes ruined credit (the #1 cause of bankruptcy in America).
- **Education + Lending → THE DEBT DIPLOMA** — the school and the loan servicer are one hand; they sold you the loan, not the future.
- **Labor + Lending → WAGE CAPTURE** — wages kept just low enough that you need the loan to live, so you work to pay the lender.
- **Housing + Labor → NO ADDRESS, NO JOB** — lose the job, lose the home; no address, no hire; the two feed each other.
- **Housing + Education → ZIP-CODE DESTINY** — school funding tied to property values; the block the Machine drained gets the worst schools.
- **Health + Labor → THE EXPENDABLE WORKER** — misclassified, no workers' comp, no benefits; get hurt and you're discarded.
- **Education + Labor → THE PIPELINE TO NOWHERE** — trained and charged for jobs that don't exist.

**The triples (a structural collapse with its own name):**
- **Housing + Lending + Labor → THE EXTRACTION ZONE** — no jobs, predatory credit, housing that builds no wealth: concentrated poverty, engineered.
- **Health + Housing + Lending → THE WIPEOUT** — medical event → debt → lose the house; a family erased in one bad year.
- **Education + Lending + Labor → THE SOLD FUTURE** — debt diploma + no jobs + more loans; paying forever for a door that was never open.

**All five → THE CAPTURED CITY** — the whole machine out in the open; generational poverty by design. That's the loss state, now wearing its real face.

**Why it's the heart of the teaching:** a pattern is *worse than its parts* — once it forms it **tightens** on its own each round, so you feel systemic harm compound the way it really does. And you break it the way you break it in real life: you don't have to fix everything — **crack the one piece the patterns share and the whole web lets go at once.** (In playtest, exposing *Lending* broke Redlining, the Medical-Debt Spiral, and the Wipeout in a single move — because Lending was the connective tissue under all three.) It proves the thesis *as you play*: you think you're fighting five separate fires, then two districts tip and the board flips a card that says **REDLINING**, and the whole table goes *"ohhh — that's what that is."* The Machine (and the Overlord) now plays to **assemble patterns**, not just capture squares.

*(Live in the prototype: `PATTERNS` fires named harms the moment the Machine grips a combo, tightens them each round, and breaks them when the block pulls any one district out of the grip.)*

## REPLAYABILITY & DEPTH (what keeps it on the table for years)

**Replayability — a different game every time:**
- **The Variable Owner.** The Underworld hides a different boss each game. It acts from round 1, face-down, so the table can *deduce* it before it's revealed — *The Algorithm* (relentless: an extra strike every round), *The Lobbyist* (leans on your strongest work; re-captures a woken district on reveal), *The Debt Buyer* (every shortcut corrupts you harder, and collections drain the Trust of anyone carrying Shady), *The Recruiter* (peels your people away — Read loss each round). Same board, a new puzzle every time. It's the Owner from the novel, as a rotating cast.
- **The Scenario deck.** Each game opens from a setup card — *The Eviction Wave, Payday Season, The Closure, The Audit, A Quiet Year* — changing the starting map and the pressure.
- **Expansion packs = the CAN domains.** Tenant Rights, Medical Debt, Immigration & Labor, Benefits. Each new pack is also a Civic Armor Network domain — **the game's content roadmap and the business's domain roadmap are the same roadmap.** The empire funds the game; the game markets the empire.
- **Legacy Campaign mode.** ~10 linked games where the block permanently changes, roles and districts unlock, and you slowly uncover who the Owner really is — ending on the novel's reveal. Sold as a premium expansion.

**Depth — what makes it art, not just clever:**
- **Shady as salvation.** Sometimes a district is one round from capture and the *only* way to save it in time is to take the shortcut — corrupt yourself to protect your people. The game forces the soul-vs-the-block choice over and over. ID8 as a gut-punch you feel every game.
- **Reach the fallen (redemption).** A flipped player isn't gone. The others can spend hard-won Trust and an action to pull them back — but it leaves the board exposed. Save the district, or save your own?
- **Layered lenses.** Red reveals the cost, amber the clock, blue the connection. A tech-tree of *seeing* — the deeper your Read, the more layers of the machine you read at once.
- **The Machine adapts.** It contests your best work (it strikes the district closest to waking), Patterns tighten on their own, and once the Underworld opens the Owner fights in the open — an extra strike and a cover-up every round. Institutions adapting to exposure, straight from the Resilience File.

**The layered lens:** Read deepens in three stages — 🔴 **RED** reveals the *cost* (and tells you trap from honest), 🟠 **AMBER** the *clock* (the deadline, your rights window), 🔵 **BLUE** the *connection* (which Underworld thread the deal feeds). Each trap's lever key says which layer you need to beat it, and blue Read is what lets you trace the Underworld and win.

*(The playable prototype `second_number_play.py` and the rules engine `sn_engine.py` implement all of it: the layered lens and levers, the five roles, the Scenario deck, the Variable Owner, corruption-as-salvation, redemption, the Underworld final act, and the Props scoreboard. `RULES.md` is the table rulebook; `test_rules.py` pins each rule to the code.)*

## THE DECK (what's actually built)
The base box is a **76-card deal deck — 12 real, documented traps and 3 honest deals per district, plus Gem City Market in Health** (Lending, Housing, Labor, Health, Education). Every trap carries the layered second number — 🔴 the real cost, 🟠 the clock, 🔵 the Underworld connection — plus a **lever key** (5 red, 4 amber, 3 blue per district). The honest deals (credit-union loan, free VITA tax prep, community land trust, union apprenticeship, state wage claim, charity care, marketplace subsidy, FAFSA, guaranteed-transfer community college…) mean blind suspicion has a cost. Every deal is a **three-way choice** — *Take it* (honest: real help; trap: fast relief now, Shady on you), *Walk* (safe on a trap; costs Trust on honest help), or *Negotiate* (spend Trust; beats a trap only if your Read reaches its lever). With 5 roles (printed as church hand fans), 12 Pattern cards, 4 Owner cards, 7 Scenario cards, 12 History cards and the Green Book, the base box is **117 cards and fans**, plus the Cookout plate and the Sou-Sou can. It grows as each CAN domain expansion adds its own traps and patterns.

**The lens, physically:** the hidden second number is printed in cyan under a red camouflage hatch — a muddy blur to the naked eye. Hold up a **red lens** (red acetate, gel, even a red candy wrapper) and the red hatch washes out while the cyan darkens: the number appears. It's the real decoder-ink trick, and the **print-and-play kit is built so it works on a home inkjet tonight.** The deluxe/manufactured edition uses proper decoder registration — and a three-filter version (red / amber / blue) where each lens layer has its own physical reveal.

*(Both exist now: `deck_cards.py` holds the full deck with levers, and `The_Second_Number_PRINT_AND_PLAY.pdf` is the 36-page West Dayton cut-and-play kit — cover, lens how-to and printer test, Grandma's reading glasses, rulebook and quick reference, the West Dayton board, five player boards, hand fans, the Cookout plate, the Sou-Sou can wrap, tokens, all 76 deals as paperwork with working reveal zones, Owner/scenario/pattern/History cards and the Green Book, a facilitator answer key, and a playtest log. Rebuild it any time with `python3 print_kit/build_print_and_play.py`.)*

## PATH TO REAL (so this isn't just a 4am dream)
Honest version — a board game's hard 80% is *playtesting*, not the idea, and the idea is the rare part you already have:

1. **Prototype cheap.** Print-and-play on paper and index cards. The red-lens trick works with a red transparency sheet and red/light-blue printing. Near-zero cost.
2. **Playtest with your built-in crew** — family, the cookout, the kids. You have the perfect test group and they'll tell you the truth.
3. **Refine the rules** over a few rounds. This is the real work, and it's fun.
4. **Fund the manufacturing without risking your own money:** **Kickstarter** is *the* path for indie board games — it proves demand and pre-sells the production run at the same time. A solid campaign funds the whole manufacturing cost up front (your 4th-and-goal with a capped downside).
5. **Manufacture** through a real board-game maker (Panda, Longpack, etc.). Minimums are usually a few hundred to ~1,000 units; the red lens is a cheap component.
6. **Or license** the concept to an existing publisher if you'd rather not run production.

**Risk to you:** low. Backers fund the box. Worst case, you made a great prototype your family loves.
