# THE SECOND NUMBER — Art direction (West Dayton Edition)

*It should look like the block on its best day: proud, loud, funny, stylish, and nobody's fool.*

This is the brief for illustrators, designers and the print kit. The print-and-play kit (`print_kit/build_print_and_play.py`) follows it as closely as a home printer allows. The manufactured edition should follow it fully, with commissioned art.

---

## 1. The feeling
- **Joy first.** The Machine is a system; it's drawn as paperwork, fine print and faceless hands. The people are drawn as people: in color, mid-laugh, mid-argument, dressed for church or for the shift.
- **Specific, not generic.** This is Black American and West Dayton culture: the stoop, the barbershop, the church fan, the cookout, the corner store's hand-painted sign. It is not generic "African" decoration; no kente borders for decoration's sake.
- **Funk, not gloom.** Dayton is the Land of Funk (the Ohio Players, Zapp & Roger, Lakeside, Slave). The game should feel like a record sleeve, not a pamphlet.

## 2. Influences (inspiration only; commission originals, never copy)
| Source | What to take from it |
|---|---|
| **AfriCOBRA**, the Chicago art collective (1968–) | "Kool-Aid colors": saturated, joyful color; lettering woven into the image. |
| **Emory Douglas** (Black Panther newspaper art) | Bold outlines, flat color, community-defense energy. |
| **Jacob Lawrence's *Migration Series*** | Flat geometric figures; storytelling in panels. Perfect for the campaign chapters. |
| **Romare Bearden's collages** | Layered paper, photos and paint. The deal cards as collaged paperwork. |
| **Quilt geometry** (Gee's Bend and family quilts) | The board as a quilt: five districts as five blocks, stitched together by the Underworld threads. Credit or partner with quilters if real patterns are used. |
| **Hand-painted signs** (corner stores, barbershop price boards, church marquees) | Display lettering and the card frames. |
| **Dayton funk record sleeves** | The box, the cover and the rulebook's energy. |

## 4. Type
- **Display:** hand-lettered, sign-painter style. For the manufactured edition, commission a lettering artist for the logo and headers. For drafts, the open-licensed (OFL) Google Fonts *Bungee* (built on urban vertical signage) or *Alfa Slab One* (poster slab) are close.
- **Body:** a warm, readable sans (*Rubik* or *Source Sans 3*, both OFL). Minimum 8 pt on cards; this is a game for grandmothers and twelve-year-olds at the same table.
- **The print kit** uses DejaVu Sans (always available, embeds cleanly) until licensed fonts are chosen.

## 5. Components as everyday objects
| Component | Looks like | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The lens** | **Grandma's reading glasses:** card frames with red film lenses | "Put your glasses on and read it again." Ties to the Elder. The print kit includes a cut-out frame. |
| **Role cards** | **Church hand fans**, with a portrait on the front and a "sponsor" panel on the back | The back's ad space carries the role's gift. |
| **Deal cards** | **Real paperwork:** leases, receipts, hospital bills, carbon-copy forms | Form numbers, a signature line ("X ________"); the second number really is in the fine print. |
| **Trust tokens** | **Bottle caps** | The print kit has a cut-out sheet; the deluxe edition uses real caps. |
| **Corruption tokens** | **Fake gold chains / casino chips** | Shiny on purpose. |
| **The Sou-Sou pot** | **A coffee can** | The hand goes around the table. |
| **The Green Book** | **A small booklet card**, green cover | Honors *The Negro Motorist Green Book*. |
| **The Cookout card** | **A paper plate** | Round card, foil-plate border. |
| **The board** | **A West Dayton map-quilt** | Five quilt blocks, landmark icons (Dunbar House, the Wright Cycle shop on West Third, Gem City Market, Wesleyan MetroPark) and the Great Miami River along the edge. |

## 6. Drawing people
- **Skin tones:** the full range of Black skin, rendered warmly and accurately; no single "Black" brown, and no grey lighting that flattens dark skin.
- **Hair:** natural hair, braids, locs, fades, waves, church hats, durags, bonnets, all drawn with care.
- **Ages and bodies:** grandmothers, teenagers, veterans, a wheelchair user, a pregnant organizer. The block is everybody.
- **Clothes:** era-correct for the campaign; for today, what people on the West Side actually wear.
- **Never:** caricature, minstrel-adjacent features, "ghetto" signifiers, trauma imagery used for shock, or the Machine drawn as Black people. The Machine is paperwork and faceless hands.

## 7. Voice
- Card voice may use African American English. It must be **written by Black writers** who speak it, and read aloud at a table before printing.
- Funny is allowed and encouraged. Preachy is not.
- Every trap stays factual. Flavor can be playful; the second number never exaggerates.

## 8. Music (optional)
A playlist card with a QR code to a Dayton funk playlist, linking to the artists' own streams rather than printing album art. Check the playlist service's terms before printing.
