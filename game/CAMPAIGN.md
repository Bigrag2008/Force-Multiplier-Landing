# THE SECOND NUMBER — Legacy campaign: *The Long Ledger*

*One family. One block. Six eras. The same Owner, wearing a different face each time.*

**Status:** a design document, being built one chapter at a time. **Chapter 3, *The Map*, is the prototype** and is built first as a standalone chapter, to prove the format before writing the other five. The rest are designs.

---

## The idea
You don't play five strangers. You play **one West Dayton family across six generations**, from Reconstruction to today. Each chapter is one or two games of the base game with that era's deals, twists and Owner.

What you win or lose carries forward: districts are rebuilt or scarred, the family's ledger grows or shrinks, and the young people of one chapter become the Elders of the next. In the final chapter the table learns what the base game only hints at: **the trap never went away; it changed clothes.**

## What carries between chapters
| Carries forward | How |
|---|---|
| **The Family Ledger** | A track from −10 to +10. Win a chapter: +2, plus +1 for each district awake at the end. Lose: −2, plus −1 for each captured district. Each Fall costs −1, each Reach earns +1. The Ledger is the family's *record*, and the final score. |
| **The community rallies** (rubber band) | The Ledger never decides how hard the next chapter is. After a **lost** chapter, the next generation starts with **+1 Trust each and one free Commons action**: the church, the club and the neighbors show up. After a **won** chapter, nothing extra. Losses are remembered, and the next game stays winnable. |
| **Board stickers** | A district awake at the end of a chapter **first heals one Scar**, if it has one; otherwise it gets a **Rebuilt** sticker (it starts later chapters at Clarity 1). A captured district gets a **Scarred** sticker (it starts at Squeeze 1). At most 2 stickers per district. Scars fade; they never vanish on their own. |
| **The lineage** | Each player names their character. Between chapters, a Student who survived becomes the next chapter's Elder (pass the Elder role to that player). Name the new generation on the lineage sheet. |
| **The History deck** | Cards are unlocked as the family lives them (below). A card unlocked in play is kept for every later game. |
| **Sealed envelopes** | Each chapter's deals, twist and Owner are in a sealed envelope. Open it only when the chapter begins. |
| **Family Decisions** | Between chapters, the family makes one permanent choice from that chapter's envelope (below). Each choice changes the next chapter's setup and is written on the lineage sheet. This is where the table argues. |
| **The Fallen, across generations** | If a family member Falls (even if they're Reached back), mark them on the lineage sheet. In a later chapter, a descendant who earns the most Props **redeems the line**: +2 on the Ledger and the mark is crossed out. |

## Where to start
Tables can start at **Chapter 2** and play Chapter 1 later as a "go back" chapter. Chapter 1 is the most emotionally heavy era, and it is a better second game than a first one.

## The chapters

### Chapter 1 — *Freedom's Ledger* (Reconstruction, 1865–1877)
- **Setting:** the family is newly free, before the move north. The chapter opens on **agency**: saving, learning to read, building a church and a school. The historian and Black writers lead this chapter's content; nothing in it is written for shock.
- **New deals:** sharecropping contracts with crop liens; the plantation store; and a deposit at the Freedman's Savings Bank. That deposit *looks* honest even through the red and amber layers; only blue Read reveals who controls it.
- **Twist:** nobody starts with Read, not even the Elder. The family learns everything at the table.
- **The Owner's face:** *The Planter's Ledger* (plays as the Debt Buyer).
- **Unlocks:** the **Sou-Sou** (mutual-aid societies) and the History card *The Freedman's Savings Bank*.
- **Family Decision:** go north now, or stay one more season and save? (Go: Chapter 2 starts in round 1 with all five districts. Stay: +1 Ledger, but Chapter 2 starts with Lending at Squeeze 1.)

### Chapter 2 — *Up North* (the Great Migration to Dayton, 1910s–1940s)
- **Setting:** the family arrives on Dayton's West Side.
- **New deals:** labor agents who promise jobs up north; boarding-house rents; company scrip.
- **Twist:** the board starts with only three districts in play. Health and Education open in round 4, when the family is settled.
- **The Owner's face:** *The Labor Agent* (plays as the Recruiter).
- **Unlocks:** the **Commons places** (the barbershop and the church first) and the History card *Dunbar, the Wrights and the Dayton Tattler*.
- **Family Decision:** put the savings into the church, the house or a child's schooling? (Church: the church gives −3 Shady in Chapter 3. House: Housing starts at Clarity 1. School: the Student starts with Read 1 everywhere.)

### Chapter 3 — *The Map* (redlining, 1930s–1960s) — **the prototype, being built first**
- **Setting:** the federal maps shade the West Side red.
- **New deals:** Housing and Lending use an era deck: contract-for-deed homes; blockbusting offers ("sell now, before the neighborhood changes"); insurance that won't write a policy for your address; a bank that won't lend below a red line. Honest era deals: a Black-owned savings and loan, a mutual-aid loan, a GI Bill home loan that a veteran actually gets.
- **Twist:** Housing and Lending start **gripped** (Squeeze 2), so REDLINING is active from round 1.
- **New action, the League:** like the Contract Buyers League, families sign a pledge to withhold together (an action and 1 Trust each). When as many families have signed as there are players, **the League forms**: Housing Squeeze −2, and every Housing and Lending trap can be negotiated with Read 1, because the families read their contracts together.
- **The Owner's face:** *The Appraiser* (plays as the Lobbyist).
- **Unlocks:** the History card *The Redlining Maps* always; *The Contract Buyers League* only if the League formed **and** REDLINING is broken at the end.
- **Family Decision:** stay and fight for the house, or move to where the map says you can? (Stay: Housing keeps its sticker and the Organizer gains +1 Props per League signature in Chapter 4. Move: remove one Scar from Housing, but the Elder role skips a generation.)

### Chapter 4 — *Voice* (civil rights and the vote, 1955–1970)
- **Setting:** the boycotts, the strikes, and Dayton electing James H. McGee as mayor in 1970.
- **New deals:** poll-tax-style fees; and "deals" offered to buy off organizers (the trap is always in blue).
- **Twist:** the Organizer's NEGOTIATE clears 3 Squeeze. Once per game the family may march: every player takes one free Commons action.
- **The Owner's face:** *The Council* (a new Owner: on reveal, it re-captures the district with the most Clarity).
- **Unlocks:** the **Cookout** and the History cards *Montgomery*, *I AM A MAN* and *Mayor James H. McGee*.
- **Family Decision:** run for office or keep organizing? (Run: the Lobbyist can't act in Chapter 5's first 3 rounds. Organize: the Organizer starts Chapter 5 with 5 Trust.)

### Chapter 5 — *The Plants Close* (1990s–2009)
- **Setting:** GM's Moraine plant closes in 2008 and NCR leaves in 2009; subprime loans flood the block.
- **New deals:** teaser-rate refinances; "cash out your equity"; temp agencies replacing the plant.
- **Twist:** uses the *The Plants Close* scenario. Before the chapter, the family chooses: **defend what you built** (every Rebuilt district is the Machine's first target each round) or **let one go** (remove one Rebuilt sticker; the Machine targets as usual). A hard choice instead of an automatic penalty.
- **The Owner's face:** *The Algorithm* (scoring, automation, risk models).
- **Unlocks:** the **Green Book** returns as an heirloom (the family remembers the old safe routes).
- **Family Decision:** sell the house at the bottom of the market, or hold on? (Sell: +2 Trust for everyone in Chapter 6, Housing loses a Rebuilt sticker. Hold: Housing keeps everything, and starts Chapter 6 at Squeeze 1.)

### Chapter 6 — *Today* (2018–)
- **Setting:** Good Samaritan Hospital closes; Gem City Market opens; and the family is still here.
- **New deals:** the whole base deck, plus Gem City Market and the honest deals.
- **Twist:** every History card the family unlocked is in play. The Elder may teach History **twice**.
- **The Owner's face:** *The Ledger Keeper*: on reveal, it shows the table every face it wore (The Planter's Ledger, the Labor Agent, the Appraiser, the Council, the Algorithm). One hand behind every era. This ending stands on its own.
- **Optional bonus envelope:** if the novel is published, a sealed bonus envelope reveals the Owner from the book. The campaign never depends on it.
- **Ending:** read the Family Ledger aloud, the lineage sheet name by name, and any redeemed Falls. The rulebook's last line applies to the whole family line: *How high did you climb before you turned into the thing you were fighting?*

## What has to be built
1. **Era deal decks:** about 12 new deals for each of chapters 1–5, written to the same three-layer standard and checked by the historian (see `COMMUNITY.md`).
2. **Engine support:** a `campaign` mode that loads a chapter's deck, twist and Owner, and saves and loads the Ledger, stickers and lineage between games. *(Chapter 3 is built first: see `sn_engine.py`, `chapter="THE_MAP"`.)*
3. **Two new Owners:** *The Council* (chapter 4) and the final Owner (chapter 6).
4. **Balance testing per chapter:** the tester runs each chapter with the expected stickers carried in from a typical previous chapter.
5. **Components:** sticker sheet, lineage sheet, Ledger track, six sealed envelopes.
6. **Sold as:** a premium expansion box, *The Long Ledger*, after the base game.
