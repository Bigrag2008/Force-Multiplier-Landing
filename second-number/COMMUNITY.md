# THE SECOND NUMBER — Doing it right

A game about a real community has to be made *with* that community. This is the plan for the people, partners, permissions and facts behind the West Dayton Edition. Treat it as a checklist before anything goes to print or to Kickstarter.

---

## 1. Ground rules
1. **Joy, not only harm.** Every hard card has a fighting-back card. The win state is a celebration.
2. **The Machine is a system, never a people.** It's drawn as paperwork and faceless hands, never as Black people, and never as any one neighborhood's residents.
3. **Nothing about us without us.** Black artists, writers and historians shape the content and are paid and credited.
4. **Every fact is checked.** A trap card that exaggerates breaks the game's promise. If a fact can't be confirmed, the card is rewritten or cut.
5. **Real names need permission.** If the game names a real business, school or program, ask first.

## 2. People to hire (paid, credited on the box)
| Role | Why |
|---|---|
| **Lead illustrator(s):** Black artists, ideally from Dayton or Ohio | The look in `ART_DIRECTION.md` depends on it. |

| **Financial-literacy reviewer** (a credit counselor or legal-aid attorney) | Signs off on every trap's red, amber and blue layer. |

## 5. Facts to verify
Each needs a source on file (an archive, a newspaper or an official site) before printing.

**Dayton**
- [ ] Gem City Market: a member-owned co-op grocery on Salem Avenue; its opening year and how membership works.
- [ ] Adventure Central at Wesleyan MetroPark: its current name and programs.
- [ ] Good Samaritan Hospital (northwest Dayton) closed in 2018.
- [ ] GM's Moraine assembly plant closed in 2008; NCR moved its headquarters out of Dayton in 2009.
- [ ] James H. McGee: Dayton's first Black mayor, 1970–1982; James H. McGee Boulevard.
- [ ] Paul Laurence Dunbar and Orville Wright were classmates at Central High School; the Wrights printed the *Dayton Tattler* in 1890.
- [ ] Dayton's neighborhoods appear on a 1930s HOLC map.
- [ ] Neighborhood names used: Wright-Dunbar, Madden Hills.
- [ ] The Funk Music Hall of Fame & Exhibition in Dayton, if it's referenced in marketing.

**History cards**
- [ ] The Freedman's Savings Bank (1865–1874): its charter, depositors and collapse.
- [ ] Maggie L. Walker: St. Luke Penny Savings Bank, 1903; the first woman to charter a U.S. bank.
- [ ] The Contract Buyers League (1968): its tactics, and the wording of its outcome.
- [ ] The Memphis sanitation strike (1968): the outcome.
- [ ] The Montgomery bus boycott: 381 days; *Browder v. Gayle*.
- [ ] The Black Panther Party's free clinics and sickle cell screening.
- [ ] Dr. Charles Drew: blood plasma storage, and his objection to separating blood by race.
- [ ] Wilberforce University: founded 1856; bought by the AME Church in 1863.
- [ ] Greenwood (1921): the massacre and the rebuilding.

**Trap cards:** a financial-literacy reviewer re-checks all 60 traps' numbers and laws for the current year, in Ohio and federally.

## 6. Playtesting in Dayton
- Run at least **10 tables** before Kickstarter: one at an HBCU class, one at a church fellowship hall, one at a barbershop, one at the library, one with families and kids, and the rest with the build crew.
- Use the playtest log in the print kit and ask its five questions every time.
- Track: did players learn a real trap? Did anyone feel talked down to? Did anyone feel the game was about *them* in a way they didn't like? Fix those before fixing balance.

## 7. Giving back (a decision for the creator)
Consider committing a share of every sale, or a number of donated copies, to West Dayton organizations: the library, a youth program, a financial-literacy nonprofit. Say so on the box only once it's real.
